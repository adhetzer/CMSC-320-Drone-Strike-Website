# Civilian Drone Strike Murders have Increased as Years Have Gone On, Independent of Drone Strike Counts: Exploring the Data Science Pipeline Through Confirmed US Drone Strikes in Afghanistan, Pakistan, Somalia, and Yemen (2010-2020)

In this paper, I use The Bureau of Investigative Journalism (TBIJ)'s [Drone Warfare dataset](https://www.thebureauinvestigates.com/explainers/our-methodology) to argue that the number of civilian deaths attributed to United States-led drone strikes in Afghanistan, Pakistan, Somalia, and Yemen have increased between 2010 to 2020 independent of the number of total drone strikes. I conclude that if the United States government values civilian life in their foreign wars, then this data supports either ignorance, incompetency, and/or a willful disregarding of that value. In any case, the trends outlined in this data suggest that the United States should withdraw from military actions in the Middle East to avoid the murder of innocent civilians.

## Educational Goals - A tutorial for a data science pipeline

This paper was written as the final project of CMSC 320 - Introduction to Data Science at UMD in the Spring 2022 semester taught by [José Manuel Calderón Trilla](http://jmct.cc/). As part of the course requirements, it also aims to explain a data science pipeline that includes (as stated on the website):

> 1. Data collection - Using The Bureau of Investigative Journalism's 2010-2020 Drone Warfare Project's CSV
> 2. Data Management - Getting our collected "Drone Warfare" data in a useable form
> 3. Exploratory Analysis - What can we figure out by looking at our managed data
> 4. Hypothesis Testing, and Analysis
> 5. Conclusions of insights gained - Civilian Drone Strike Murders have Increased as Years Have Gone On, Independent of Drone Strike Counts

Actionable insights and key terms are presented in **bold** text for ease of reference. We also include links to documentation or further reading throughout the paper.

## Methodology

This website was built from HTML generated by a `Jupyter Notebook`. The libraries used for data analysis include:

- `pandas` for data collection, management, tidying, and exploration
- `seaborn` violin plots and `pandas` scatter plots for data visualization
- `statsmodels` and `pandas` for statistical analysis

We use a z-test to test our hypothesis that there is positive correlation between the year and the minimum total people killed (found via covariance).
